The Island Water Mystery

What happens to matter when it seems to disappear?

Theo has reached a part of his island survival book that should make sense—but it doesn’t. The story’s water trick seems to work, but the explanation is missing. There’s only one way to find out whether it really adds up: follow the evidence.

Arizona science standard

5.P1U1.1 — Analyze and interpret data to explain that matter of any type can be subdivided into particles too small to see and, in a closed system, if properties change or chemical reactions occur, the amount of matter stays the same.

Student-friendly learning goals

  • I can use measurements and observations to track matter even when I cannot see it.
  • I can analyze data to explain why the amount of matter stays the same in a closed system.
  • I can create a model showing what happened to the water and salt.
Instructional boundary: Students are not expected to distinguish mass from weight or explain evaporation, condensation, or chemical reactions at the atomic scale.
Story start

The part Theo read twice

Theo's finger stopped halfway down the page.

He read the same sentence again.

“The family used the sun to turn ocean water into fresh water.”

Mom looked up from the kitchen table.

“You read that twice,” she said.

Theo tapped the sentence.

“Because it skipped something.”

He turned back to the beginning of the chapter.

Theo turned the page.

A new chapter had already begun.

He flipped back.

Then forward again.

“That’s it?”

Theo's question

The story skipped something

Theo carried the open book to the kitchen table.

“The story jumps from the setup to ‘It worked,’” he said.

He pushed the book toward Mom.

“You’re the chemist. Does this make sense to you?”

Mom studied the illustration.

“Science could explain this.”

Theo looked up.

“Then what happened?”

Mom slid the book back toward him.

“That’s the part you get to figure out. What does the story leave unanswered?”

Theo pointed to the container.

“The water around the cup got lower, and then water showed up in the empty cup.”

He flipped back a page.

“But the ocean water was salty. What happened to the salt?”

Mom nodded toward the beginning and ending pictures.

“What else would you need to know?”

Theo studied them.

“How much they started with. How much they ended with. Otherwise, how do we know where everything went?”

Mom waited.

Theo looked at the page again.

“Evidence from before and after.”

Starting the trail

Start with the evidence

Mom cleared a space on the table.

“Then don’t begin with an answer,” she said. “Begin by keeping track.”

Theo pulled his notebook closer.

“If we figure this out, I want to write to the author.”

Mom raised an eyebrow.

“About the story?”

“About the science,” Theo said. “The part the story skipped.”

Mom opened the notebook to a fresh page.

“Then make sure the evidence can support what you tell them.”

Theo picked up his pencil.

Mom tapped the blank page.

“Start with what you think happened. Later, see what the evidence does to your idea.”

Setting up the test

Before anything changes

Mom set the digital scale in the center of the counter.

Beside it, she placed the water, salt, measuring tools, clear container, small collection cup, clear cover, pebble, and stirring spoon.

Theo moved his notebook beside the scale.

“Before we change anything,” he said, “we need to know what we started with.”

Mom slid the measuring cup toward the scale.

“Water first?”

Theo nodded and uncapped his pen.

The scale was ready.
